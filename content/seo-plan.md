# publicspeakingtips.mx: SEO content plan

One-page site, Mexican Spanish (es-MX), ranking target: the English query **"public speaking tips"** searched from Mexico. Companion file: `copy.json` (final copy, sources, JSON-LD, fact ledger). Copy is generated by `build-copy.mjs` and verified by `check-copy.mjs` (both in this folder; the checker reads the fact repositories from the session scratchpad, so point `facts_flat.json` / `pos_stats.json` paths at wherever those files live if you re-run it later).

## 1. Keyword strategy

### Why an English keyword can rank on an es-MX page
Mexican searchers mix languages constantly ("tips", "public speaking", "coaching", "networking" are everyday loanwords). Google serves results for the query as typed, ranked by relevance to the query language *and* the searcher's locale. A page whose title, H1, URL and lead carry the exact English phrase, whose body is in the searcher's language, and whose `hreflang`/`inLanguage` say es-MX, is the best-fit document for that intersection (English query, Mexico, Spanish reader). The English-language competition for this query is not localized to Mexico; the Spanish-language competition does not carry the English phrase. We sit in the gap.

### Risk
- Google may classify the page as Spanish and treat "public speaking tips" as a minor cross-language entity match. Mitigation: keep the exact phrase in the highest-weight slots (title, H1, URL root, first sentence, one H2) and leave everything else in Spanish so the page never reads as spammy.
- Thin SERP demand: the English query from Mexico is a fraction of "consejos para hablar en público". Mitigation: the Spanish variants below are woven in naturally so the page also ranks for them.
- Do not over-repeat the English phrase. Four verbatim placements in body-level slots is the ceiling; the rest of the page uses Spanish equivalents.

## 6. Freshness and risk notes

**Dated / rolling facts**
- The Confidently numbers (8,184 sessions, +6.6%, 6.22 to 6.63, 20 to 59 s, 5.52 to 6.68) come from the sibling page dated 2026-07-20. They will drift as sessions accumulate; re-pull from the sibling page whenever it updates and bump `dateModified`.
- Speaking-rate bands (VirtualSpeech, 100th Monkey, TED) are English-language measurements. The copy says so explicitly ("medido en inglés") and points the reader to their own 1-minute recording. Spanish syllable rate is different; no Spanish-language wpm fact exists in the repository.
- PMC prevalence figures (77%, 15% to 30%) are literature-review numbers from older primary studies; stable, but cite the PMC ids as done rather than "a recent study".

**"kb" facts used without a URL (no citation attached in copy; listed in the ledger as "kb (sin URL)")**
- 5 Ps of preparation (#14455025 / #36513864)
- 4-7-8 breathing steps (#19472442); the technique name is backed by schoolcounselor.org #58752, which is what the copy links.
- 3 to 5 seconds of eye contact per person (#40991370); MSU's page gives 4-5 s (S#63596431), so the range is consistent.
- "Above 180 wpm comprehension drops" (web:933152367:30:web:9)
- 50%/70% split of the 50/70 rule (S#16922169, no URL); the rule itself is cited to MSU and the 50%-while-speaking half to Verywell Mind.

**Weaker-authority sources deliberately kept (because they are the only source for a named framework the FAQ must answer)**
- 3:2:1 rule: reddit r/ConnectBetter thread (#52378 etc.). The "1" of 3:2:1 is not defined anywhere in the repository, so the copy describes only the 3 and the 2 and never invents the 1.
- 5 C's: LinkedIn (Joe Yazbeck) and vistaprojects.com.
- Vocal 5 Ps: lemonadeday.org.
- Rule #1 opinions: the-rheumatologist.org, genardmethod.com, LinkedIn (Sinek).
- "5 minutes a day" SMART goal: medium.com (S#63671915). Low authority; it is framed as a goal, not a research result. Drop it if you want a stricter source bar (the sentence still works as "una meta realista").

**Deliberately excluded**
- "People fear public speaking more than death" and the 1973 Bruskin survey (#15196 flags it as a misinterpretation).
- 40% NIMH figure (S#20753252, S#1490907): attributed to NIMH second-hand via nationalsocialanxietycenter.com and Gallup 2001; no NIMH URL in the repository.
- 75% / 73% / 40 million / 15 million / 200 million / 25 million glossophobia figures: many rows, conflicting, mostly from counseling-marketing blogs (ambitionsaba, crossrivertherapy, novoresume). The PMC 77% and 15% to 30% cover the same ground with a citable URL.
- Wage penalty 10% / promotion 15% (talks.co, novoresume, crossrivertherapy): no primary source in the repository.
- 44% women / 37% men, 12.2% vs 5.8% (grandrisingbehavioralhealth, crowncounseling): secondary; gender framing adds nothing to a tips page.
- Only 8% seek help (crowncounseling): true-sounding but a negative frame; excluded under the positive-frames rule.
- Propranolol dosages (lifestance, etc.): medical dosing has no place on a tips page.
- "27 seconds to make a first impression", "16% trust increase (Mehrabian and Wiener 1967)", "70% of personality traits (1988 study)": kb rows with no URL and shaky provenance.
- Mehrabian 55/38/7: not in the repository at all, and it is routinely misquoted; left out on purpose.
- The `mx` rows of pos_stats.json: all 200 are noise (Spanish-speaking clinics in the US, New Mexico anxiety counts, Chicana identity, etc.); none used.
- AmberWillo's "goes live December 2026" date from the sibling page: not restated, so the page does not carry a launch promise it cannot control. Tip 3 only says AmberWillo is the English sibling that practices in front of real people.
- Box breathing (4-4-4-4, kb #40823238): cut in the length trim; the 4-7-8 technique covers the same need with a linked source.

**Facts wanted but not found in the repository**
- Any Spanish-language or Mexico-specific public speaking statistic (prevalence in Mexico, INEGI/UNAM/Tec de Monterrey data, Spanish wpm norms). Nothing usable exists; the page's Mexican identity rests on language, not local data.
- A sourced figure for stories/humor (recall, retention, engagement lift) for tip 8: none.
- A sourced figure for visual aids (retention, attention) for tip 9: none; only qualitative Pitt/Harvard/Babson statements.
- A sourced filler-word statistic with a URL: the repository has "every 12 seconds" (S#261507), "5% to 8% of words" (S#51633408, Erard), "6% of spontaneous speech" (S#51134581) and "Sinek under 1%" (S#37749604), all without URLs. Not used; would strengthen tip 1 and tip 10 if a citable source turns up.
- A definition of the "1" in the 3:2:1 rule.
- Primary NIMH or Gallup URL for the 40% figure.

**Product note (not in the copy)**
The Confidently test at `https://quiz.confidently.pro/onboarding?ref=publicspeakingtips.mx` currently runs in English (prompt words, UI and scoring feedback). The copy describes the mechanics honestly (two random words, 30 seconds, voice and face scored, fillers counted, one thing to fix) without claiming a Spanish interface. If a Spanish version ships, add "en español" to the CTA line; until then, consider a one-line note next to the button in the template ("La prueba está en inglés por ahora") so the click is informed.
